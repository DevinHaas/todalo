Todalo uses a handful of words in a particular way. Some are familiar from other to-do apps; others, such as Season, Horizon and Ritual, are Todalo's own. This page explains each one in a sentence or two, grouped by where you'll meet it.

## Tasks and capture

### Sub-task

A task inside another task. Use sub-tasks to break a bigger task into steps.

### Recurrence

The repeating schedule on a task: every so many days, weeks, months or years, with an optional end date. A task with a recurrence comes back after you complete it.

### Anchor date

What a recurring task counts from when it works out its next date. It's either the task's due date (**Scheduled date**), so the task keeps a steady rhythm, or the day you completed it (**Completed date**), so the next one is always a set time after you last did it.

### Smart date recognition

Dates, times and repeats typed into a task's name, such as `tomorrow at 4pm` or `every day`, are recognised and turned into the task's date instead of staying in the name. The recognised words are highlighted while you type; click them to keep them as plain text.

### Ramble session

Speaking your tasks instead of typing them. Todalo turns what you say into task cards as you talk. Nothing is saved until you finish the session and add the tasks; if you discard it, nothing changes.

## Planning your time

### Time block

A task with a start time and a length, shown as a block on your schedule in the colour of its goal. Dragging across an empty slot creates one; moving or resizing the block changes the task's times.

### Horizon

Where a task without a time waits to be planned. **Now** holds the next things to plan. **Next** is where a task goes once it's sorted. **Later** is parked: still listed but muted, and the Coach won't propose it until you name it or it comes due. A scheduled task keeps its horizon.

### Unsorted

An Inbox task that has no horizon yet. Sorting a task means giving it a horizon, a project, or both. A project task without a horizon counts as Next.

### Stale

An open task with no date, not parked in Later, that nobody has touched for 30 days or more.

## Goals and Seasons

### Goal

An outcome you're working towards, and the top level of how your work is organised. A goal can be served by many projects, and one task can count towards several goals. Goals are optional: if you hide them, projects and tasks work just the same.

### Season

A stretch of time your goals are planned and measured against: 12 weeks for a long Season, 4 weeks for a short sprint, or a length you choose. A goal can carry on across several Seasons, with a target set for each one.

### Primary goal and secondary goal

A goal's tier within one Season. Primary goals get your best time and are protected; secondary goals are the first to be postponed when time runs short. Around three primary goals per Season is a good guide, but the real limit is how much time they need.

### Milestone

A task marked as a milestone for a goal, with done criteria. It counts as reached only once every criterion is met.

### Done criteria

The specific, checkable conditions on a milestone that say when it's reached.

### Goal status

Where a goal stands in its Season: **On track**, **Adjust** or **Off track**. Todalo works it out from your progress ratings, reached milestones and the goal's tasks you've completed or scheduled. Neither you nor the Coach sets it by hand.

### Progress rating

Your own judgement, given during a review, of how far a period moved you towards a goal: *A massive amount*, *Solid*, *Mixed*, *Little progress* or *Not relevant*. You can add a few words, written or spoken.

### Energy rating

Whether a task or calendar event was **Energizing** or **Draining** for you that time. You rate each occurrence separately, because energy changes with mood and circumstance, and recent ratings count for more when Todalo learns when certain work suits you best.

## Rituals

### Rituals

The Check-in, the Evening review, the Weekly review and the Season review. Each one is optional: you choose which you do, and goals, time blocks and the Coach all work without them.

### Check-in

The morning Ritual for planning the day. It suggests Inbox, overdue and due-today tasks to schedule today or give a date, then shows today's schedule so you can adjust it.

### Evening review

The evening Ritual where you rate today's progress on each goal in the current Season, with optional notes.

### Weekly review

The weekly Ritual where you check each goal's status for the Season and plan the coming week.

### Season review

The Ritual at the end of a Season where you look back on it, decide which goals continue, and set their targets for the next Season.

### Ritual notification

The notice that a Ritual is ready, such as "Your Evening review is ready". It appears once at the Ritual's time while the Ritual is on and not done or skipped. Click it to open the Ritual. You can turn these notifications off.

## The Coach

### Coach

The AI in Todalo that helps you plan, reflect and stay on your primary goals. The Coach never changes anything directly: everything it proposes arrives as a draft.

### Draft

A set of changes the Coach proposes to your tasks and schedule: tasks added, moved or removed. Drafts show as dashed, provisional items until you accept or decline them, and nothing touches your calendar until you accept.

### Pattern

Something the Coach has learned about how you work, such as "ships best in the mornings" or "admin is draining". You can always see your patterns, and edit or delete any of them.

### Tidy-up

A request to the Coach to go through your stale tasks. It drafts one change per task, each with a reason: park it in Later, delete it, complete it, move it, or keep it. Keeping a task changes nothing but counts it as fresh for another 30 days.
